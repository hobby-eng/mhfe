MHFE 0.6.0 implements suites `MHFE-BIP39-256-EXPERIMENTAL-3` and `MHFE-BIP39-LP-EXPERIMENTAL-4` and
the optional profiles MHFE-REPAIR-1, MHFE-PASSWORD-CHECK-1 and MHFE-WALLET-CHECK-SEED-1, as defined
by [specification v0.5.0](https://github.com/hobby-eng/mhfe-spec/releases/tag/v0.5.0), the same specification as
MHFE 0.5.0, with three application rules of its next revision, not yet released. A rekey shows
every user a warning about the wallets that other passwords open on the old container, where
v0.5.0 required a question about them. In every recovery the short lengths' built-in checks take
precedence over a length the owner states, where v0.5.0 rejected a stated length whose check
failed and confirmed a rekey only at the length the owner states; a rekey with the length detected
is confirmed by an address, the fingerprint or the owner, never by the built-in check alone. Every
recovery tests each
24-word reading with the 16-bit check of `MHFE-WALLET-CHECK-SEED-1`, which v0.5.0 left to
applications that offer the profile. Neither suite changes: containers
made with versions 0.4.0 and 0.5.0 stay readable. Version 0.5.0 stays published as it is; these
notes list what changed after it.

## New

- **Checks at every start.** Before a command that handles a secret asks for anything (`new`,
  `encrypt`, `decrypt`, `check`, `rekey`, `wallets`, `repair`, `repair-words` and `password`), and
  once before the start menu opens, every part of the program is compared with known answers, each
  with a case it must refuse, and core dumps, the kernel's restrictions and locked memory are read
  back, in a few hundredths of a second. A part that gives another answer stops the command before
  its first question with exit code 1, and so do core dumps that cannot be turned off.
  `mhfe serve`, which handles no secret, tests only the hashes.
- **A chosen word in a new phrase.** `mhfe new` asks whether you want to choose one word of the new
  phrase, at a position or anywhere, typed on the private screen, and one word it must never hold
  (`--never-use`). Every phrase that meets the wishes is equally likely. Before it draws it says how
  many of the 256 random bits remain for someone who knows the word: from 240 the phrase still has
  far more than enough, and below it, down to 228.98 with the wallet check, it is allowed but not
  recommended. It also warns that someone who learns or guesses the chosen word can rule out almost
  every wrong password with it and tell the wallet from a decoy; the README shows an example and
  advises against choosing a word. A program feature, not part of the specification.
- **Word hints.** Wherever words are typed, a hint below the line lists the words of the list that
  begin with the word being typed: from two letters the words, after one letter how many there are,
  and a word no list has is said. Tab completes a word and Ctrl+W deletes one. Seed phrases,
  container phrases, repair words and the chosen word use the BIP39 list; passwords the EFF list,
  where Tab stays part of the password. Hints appear only where the typed text is shown.
- `mhfe self-test` runs the tests of the start together with slower ones, in a few seconds and with
  up to 256 MiB, and shows one line for each part: the rounds of all 27 published MHFE vectors,
  every Unicode character against the password rule, 1,024 bytes of the system's random generator
  and the terminal's echo among them. In 0.5.0 it ran only the two published vectors at full cost.
- `mhfe self-test --vectors` adds those two published vectors at full cost, and when a result is
  not as published it says where the work first left the published path: before Argon2id or in
  it, naming the round, or after its last call. The start menu's entry asks which of the two tests
  to run.
- `mhfe decrypt`, `check`, `rekey` and `wallets` take `?` in the container phrase and ask at once
  for its repair words; words that are not a container offer to type them again or repair them.
  Every repaired word is shown before the repaired container phrase is used, and `--repair` asks
  for the card right after the container phrase, also in a script.
- Without the repair words, those commands, and `mhfe repair` too, search for words typed as `?`: by
  the container's own fingerprint or address, up to two words on every core of the processor, an
  address among the first 20 receiving and change addresses or as far as `--scan-gap` says, and with
  the password by the fingerprint or an address of the owner's wallet, or by the original seed
  phrase's own checks with its BIP39 passphrase or without, one word, each candidate a full
  recovery, the time said first. After a refusal the question comes again, not the container phrase.
  The library's `search` module holds the search, with its self-check `container-search`, and the
  tool's one search module serves every command; `mhfe repair` asks the settings for it only once
  such a search is chosen (`--pim`, `--mem`, `--scan-gap`).
- The question about the length of the original seed phrase offers "Detect automatically" last, and
  `--words auto` does the same, in `mhfe check` under the built-in check and in `mhfe rekey`;
  `mhfe decrypt` takes `--words auto` as its default. `check` then asks whether a BIP39 passphrase
  is used with the original seed phrase, which finds a 24-word phrase drawn with the phrase +
  passphrase check too, and when no length is found asks how many words it has: for 24 words it
  compares an address or the fingerprint on the same recovery, without the rounds again. `rekey`
  confirms a detected length by an address, the fingerprint or the phrase shown, as for 24 words,
  since a 24-word phrase can pass a shorter phrase's check by chance (AUD-017-FUN001), and asks for
  the length when several pass by accident. It recovers the old container once: a confirmation
  that is refused is followed by another without the rounds again (AUD-017-UI002).
- Answers that are not secret are options too: `--repair-words N` (0 for none) in `mhfe encrypt`,
  `new` and `rekey`, `--passphrase-used yes|no` in `decrypt` and `rekey`, and `--confirm
check|address|fingerprint|show` in `rekey`. A script gives `decrypt` the BIP39 passphrase for the
  16-bit check on a third line with `--passphrase-used yes` (AUD-017-UI001).
- `mhfe encrypt`, `mhfe new` and `mhfe rekey` offer, where the new password is set, the user's
  own or one that MHFE makes, as `mhfe password` does: five dice words, five words and a check word,
  or sixteen random characters, shown once and typed back from the copy (`--new-password`).
- `mhfe encrypt` names any BIP39 passphrase of the wallet in what to keep, since MHFE encrypts only
  the phrase. It does not ask whether the wallet has one, at a terminal or in a script.
- `mhfe rekey` asks once whether the wallet has a BIP39 passphrase, whatever confirms the recovery,
  before any address or fingerprint is typed and before the long work, and names it in what to
  keep. After an address or the fingerprint it asks for the passphrase only of a wallet that has
  one, and refuses an empty one.
- The question of `mhfe rekey` whether the wallet has a BIP39 passphrase has no default: in the list
  no answer is marked, the hint says "Enter selects once one is marked", and Enter does nothing
  until an arrow key marks one, a digit choosing at once; the numbered form asks "Choice:" and
  refuses an empty line.
- The summary of a command says whether the secrets typed are kept in locked memory, out of swap,
  or warns when the system refused to lock it.
- The kernel restrictions that a command's summary reports are confirmed by trying what each one
  forbids; one that the kernel reported but that does not hold is left out of the summary, with a
  warning.
- A prompt for a secret refuses to read when the terminal did not turn its echo off: "The terminal
  did not turn its echo off; no secret was read."

## Changed

- Every recovery tests each 24-word reading with the 16-bit check of a phrase drawn with it, as the
  next revision of the specification requires: the container does not show whether a phrase was
  made with the check. `mhfe decrypt` asks whether a BIP39 passphrase is used with the phrase when
  a 24-word reading comes out, says why, and reports whether the reading passes. `mhfe check` and
  `mhfe rekey` report it with the passphrase given, or none; it never confirms a rekey. In the
  browser package, `decrypt()` takes `passphrase` and gives each reading `walletCheck`, in place
  of `passesWalletCheckWithoutPassphrase`, and a rekey reports `walletCheck` beside its result.
- A stated length no longer replaces detection, in every recovery (the next revision of the
  specification). A built-in check that passes takes precedence over the length you state, and
  MHFE tells you. A stated 12- to 21-word length that no check passes is still refused. 24 stated
  words beside a shorter length that passes give both readings, the checked one first. A rekey
  whose check finds another length than the one stated asks for a receiving address or the master
  key fingerprint, or shows the phrase, before it encrypts again (AUD-015-FUN001). The browser
  package gives each reading `statedWords` and `otherLengths`, and refuses such a rekey under the
  built-in check alone with `LENGTH_DIFFERS`; its owner callback gets `statedWords` too
  (AUD-017-API001).
- `mhfe new` marks no answer of its question about the wallet check, which preselected "No check"
  in 0.5.0: Enter alone does nothing there, also when typed ahead, until an arrow key marks an
  answer.
- More of the phrase stays in locked memory, out of swap, on Linux and macOS: the entropy and the
  state of an encryption from before its first round to the end of its check, both halves of the
  state through every round with the message of each round that holds one of them, the state that
  `decrypt`, `check`, `rekey` and `wallets` recover, every reading of it that `check` compares with
  an address or the fingerprint, and the normalized BIP39 passphrase a seed is made with. Each is
  locked before anything is written into it.
- In a script, `mhfe check --address` without `--coin` still compares with a Bitcoin address, as
  in 0.5.0, but now says so: the summary records "Bitcoin, as no --coin was given", and an address
  that is not Bitcoin's is refused with a message that names Bitcoin and `--coin`, where it was
  refused only as malformed.
- A usage error, such as an unknown option, is one red "✗ Error:" line followed by clap's tip and
  the usage in grey, exit code 2, instead of clap's "error:" format.
- Error lines and the facts of a summary, such as what to keep, wrap at 78 columns.
- The help of every command has an Examples section, `new`, `wallets`, `repair` and
  `repair-words` included, and every help line fits 80 columns; the longer explanations of some
  options moved from `-h` to `--help`.
- The README's table of exit codes names more ways a command is cancelled with 130 than Ctrl+C:
  Escape or q at a list, Ctrl+D on the empty prompt of a secret, SIGTERM and a closed terminal.
  Ctrl+\\ and Ctrl+Z outside a prompt or a list end the tool with 130 too, as Ctrl+C does (see
  Fixed).
- `--words 0` detects the length as `--words auto` does, as the browser package's `words: 0`, and
  `--scan-gap` takes 1 to 2^31 addresses, as the package and the library do: 0 was accepted and
  then ignored or refused late.
- `mhfe decrypt` refuses a `--words` that the container cannot have before it asks for the
  password, as `check` and `rekey` do.
- `mhfe check --words 24` and a check at 24 words in the browser give the library's refusal: "A
  24-word original seed phrase has no built-in check (only 12, 15, 18 or 21 words have one)", still
  `INVALID_WORD_COUNT`; the wallet check of a phrase that is not 24 words long says that only a
  24-word phrase has one, where both said that a phrase must have 12 to 24 words.
- Two passwords that differ are refused in the library's words, "The password and its repetition
  differ", by every command and in the browser.
- `mhfe rekey` no longer asks whether the funds of wallets that other passwords open on the old
  container are moved or backed up another way. Nothing is lost at that step, as the old container
  keeps opening every such wallet, so one sentence at the top of the container prompt says instead:
  wallets that other passwords open on the old container do not move to the new one, so keep the old
  container, its passwords, and a PIM or memory level that is not 0, until you have moved their
  funds (the owner's decision, in place of AUD-007-FUN002's question). The tool no longer advises
  destroying the old container: whether to keep it is the owner's choice.

## Fixed

- A browser page's call that was still waiting for its class's startup check could run although a
  full self-check had failed meanwhile; it is now refused with `SELF_CHECK_FAILED`, as every call
  after a failed check is (AUD-014-SEC001).
- The chosen-word filter kept the 24 word numbers of each drawn phrase, and the lower-case copy of
  a chosen word, in memory that was not wiped; both are wiped now, and the wishes name no word in
  `Debug` (AUD-014-SEC002).
- Firefox crashed the page when every worker of a new phrase's draw failed within milliseconds,
  for example from a broken random source: the other workers were terminated while they still
  loaded the WebAssembly. A worker that is still loading is now terminated once it has loaded it.
  Found by the multi-chain wallet tools' browser tests.
- The phrase + passphrase check of `mhfe check` (the wallet check of MHFE-WALLET-CHECK-SEED-1)
  accepted an empty BIP39 passphrase and also compared the 12- to 21-word readings of a 24-word
  container, so that a passphrase found to pass with a shorter reading could report a match. It
  now asks for a passphrase that is not empty, asks again after an empty one, and compares the
  24-word reading only, as the profile defines the check.
- A refused master key fingerprint, derivation path or coin no longer repeats what was typed: a seed
  phrase pasted into the fingerprint field by mistake was printed in the error (AUD-016-SEC001).
- Cancelling a search for missing container words no longer waits until the address search of the
  candidate under way has finished: the search stops between two addresses (AUD-016-API001).
- The startup summary said typed secrets were kept in locked memory after locking a small probe,
  while the 8 KiB buffer of a typed line could not be locked under a low limit. The probe now locks
  a buffer of that size, and a line whose buffer cannot be locked says so (AUD-016-SEC002).
- A wide character in a password, such as a CJK one or an emoji, put the cursor in the wrong column
  on deletion and at the end of a row. Combining marks and the Hangul Jamo vowels and final
  consonants now take no cell, and invisible formatting characters such as U+202E are no longer
  shown (AUD-016-UI001, AUD-018).
- The words found by a search for missing container words were formatted into memory that was not
  wiped (AUD-017-SEC001).
- Ctrl+\\, Ctrl+Z, SIGTERM and a closed terminal (SIGHUP) end the tool as Ctrl+C does, with exit
  code 130, after the terminal is restored and the private screen left; in 0.5.0 they could leave a
  secret on the screen (AUD-015-SEC004). At a prompt or a list, Ctrl+\\ and Ctrl+Z are typed keys
  instead. They are read back as routed to Ctrl+C's handler, and the tool says when they are not.
- Folder and file names, the name of a swap device and a mistyped argument in a usage error were
  written to the terminal as they were, so that a name could send it control sequences. The tool,
  `mhfe serve` and `mhfe-fast-mode.py` now show every control character escaped, such as `\u{1b}`,
  and refuse a page name in `mhfe-fast-mode.sha256` that holds one (AUD-015-SEC002, AUD-018).
- A malformed reply of a worker threw in the page and left the operation running; it now ends the
  operation with `PACKAGE_MISMATCH` (AUD-016-API002). An explicit `null` for a secret is a
  `TypeError`, as the declarations say, instead of an empty text (AUD-016-API003).
- A command refused for want of a terminal names `--stdin` only when the command has that option;
  otherwise it says to run the command in a terminal.
- A password refused while it is encoded, and a line typed at a secret's prompt that ends in an
  error, such as one too long, are wiped before their memory is unlocked, no longer after.

## Browser package

- The package offers everything the command-line tool does except what only an operating system or a
  terminal gives: memory levels above 0, the private screen, the process protections, script mode,
  the vector replays and the benchmarks. `docs/BROWSER-PACKAGE.md` lists each with the reason.
- It is a set of independent module classes over one shared WebAssembly and worker, each usable
  alone: the core (`MhfeClient`), repair words (`MhfeRepair`), the password tools (`MhfePasswords`)
  and the wallet tools (`MhfeWallet`), with a small shared runtime.
- Its files lie in the folders `runtime/`, `core/`, `repair/`, `passwords/` and `wallet/` instead of
  side by side as `client.js`, `mhfe-worker.js`, `mhfe_core_bg.wasm` and the Argon2 builds;
  `modules.json` lists each module's files with their SHA-256. Every class takes `workerSource`, the
  text of `runtime/worker.js`, and `wasm`, `runtime/mhfe.wasm`, which `coreWasm` was.
- `MhfeWallet.drawPhrase()` takes `chosen` and `neverUse`, and `MhfeWallet.describeDraw()` says
  what they cost before the draw; `parameters()` gives `maxChosenWords`, `maxNeverUseWords` and
  `recommendedRandomBits`, and the new code is `INVALID_WORD_WISH`.
- `MhfeWallet.wordHints()` and `MhfePasswords.wordHints()` give the hint below a line of BIP39
  words or a password being typed, by the command-line tool's rule.
- `MhfeClient.rekey()` puts the phrase of a container into a new one under a new password or new
  settings, in 36 rounds, once its recovery is confirmed by the built-in check, an address, the
  fingerprint or the owner. After `AMBIGUOUS_LENGTH` or `LENGTH_DIFFERS` its `onConfirmAgain` gives
  another confirmation, judged on the same recovery without the rounds again, as the command-line
  tool asks again (AUD-017-UI002).
- `MhfeClient.openHiddenWallets()` opens a session of hidden wallets on a 24-word container: each
  `open()` gives the wallet of a new password, and `close()` overwrites the session's passwords and
  passphrase.
- `MhfeClient.selfTest()` encrypts the published suite 3 vector and recovers the suite 4 vector at
  full cost, and says where a result first left the published path (`fault`).
- `encrypt()` takes `repairWordCount`, whether the wallet has a BIP39 passphrase where the page
  knows it (`walletHasPassphrase`, optional) and the choice of a check word review
  (`passwordRepair`), and resolves to a sealed container with its fingerprint, built-in check, other
  lengths, repair words and the list of what to keep (`keep`). Without `walletHasPassphrase` the
  list names any passphrase of the wallet (`passphraseIfAny`), as `mhfe encrypt` does.
- `decrypt()` takes `passphrase` and gives each candidate a `status` ("verified", "noBuiltInCheck",
  "readAs24" or "readAs24Chosen"), its fingerprint without a passphrase
  (`fingerprintWithoutPassphrase`), for 24 words whether it passes the 16-bit wallet check with
  that passphrase, or with none (`walletCheck`), and `statedWords` and `otherLengths`.
- `check()` also takes `{ walletCheck: true }`, the phrase + passphrase check of a 24-word
  container, which needs a BIP39 passphrase that is not empty.
- `readPhrase()` and `readContainer()` say what the page needs before an operation: the containers a
  phrase can go into, the lengths a container's phrase can have, what confirms a recovery of each
  and whether hidden wallets and the wallet check apply; every class's `parameters()` gives its
  fixed values.
- Progress reports its stage: "encrypt", "check", "recover" or "compare", "search" for a search
  for missing words, and "draw" for a new phrase.
- `MhfeRepair` makes repair words for a container (`repairWords()`), repairs a container phrase
  with them (`repairContainer()`) and says what a container phrase as typed is
  (`inspectContainer()`), so that a page asks for the repair words where the command-line tool
  does. `MhfeClient.searchCandidates()`, `searchDecoy()` and `searchWallet()` search for missing
  words without them, as the command-line tool does.
- `MhfePasswords` reviews a password with a check word (`review()`), estimates its strength
  (`strength()`) and makes passwords of words, of five words and their check word, or of characters
  (`make()`). A `count` with the kind "checkWord" is refused (a `TypeError`, and `INVALID_REQUEST`
  in the WebAssembly), as `mhfe password` refuses `--check-word` with `--words`.
- `MhfeWallet` gives the wallet check of a phrase (`walletCheck()`), master key fingerprints
  (`fingerprint()`), what an address check will search (`describeAddress()`) and new 24-word
  phrases, with the wallet check if asked, drawn in several workers (`drawPhrase()`).
- Every class checks its parts with known answers before its first operation (`startupCheck()`) and
  on request with slower cases (`fullCheck()`); a part that fails closes the class for good
  (`SELF_CHECK_FAILED`). Every Argon2 operation also checks its Argon2 build before its first round
  and after its last.
- Every file a page loads, the Argon2 builds included, carries a build identifier derived from all
  of them, and files of different builds are refused with `PACKAGE_MISMATCH`, such as "The file
  core/argon2-st.js is of build … and runtime/worker.js of build …: take every file of the package
  from one build."
- The package identifies itself by its release version, `version` in every `parameters()` and
  `packageVersion()` in the WebAssembly, instead of the counter `apiVersion`, which was 7 in 0.5.0.
- New error codes reach a page: those of the library listed under [Library](#library), and of the
  package alone `PACKAGE_MISMATCH` and `SESSION_CLOSED` (a session of hidden wallets used after
  it ended). `PASSWORDS_DIFFER` and `PASSPHRASES_DIFFER` (a new wallet's passphrase and its
  repetition differ) are now codes of the library too, and the classes give their early
  refusals in the library's words.
- `check()` requires the `coin` of an address, which defaulted to "bitcoin" in 0.5.0, so that a page
  for one coin names no other.
- Phrases reach the WebAssembly as UTF-8 bytes, which are transferred to the worker and wiped there
  like passwords; as text they left copies in its memory. A phrase with an unpaired surrogate is
  therefore refused with `INVALID_PASSWORD_TEXT` instead of `INVALID_PHRASE`.
- A `Uint8Array` the page passes is always copied into an array of its own: a subclass whose
  `slice()` gives a view, such as Node's `Buffer`, was transferred and emptied.
- A password or its repetition of the wrong type is a `TypeError`, also when both are the same
  value, instead of `PASSWORDS_DIFFER`.
- A new password typed twice is checked by the library, its own rules first and then the
  comparison, as the command-line tool checks it, so that both give the same refusal: a password
  with a tab and a repetition that differs is `CONTROL_CHARACTER_IN_PASSWORD`, no longer
  `PASSWORDS_DIFFER`, and an empty password `EMPTY_PASSWORD` from the WebAssembly.
- `readPhrase().containers` gives each container its own `otherLengths`, empty for a same-length
  one, as the command-line tool warns of other lengths only for a 24-word container.
- `parameters()` gives `decoyScanGap`, the default of `searchDecoy()`, and `argon2Parts`, the
  self-check's Argon2 parts, which the classes and the worker took from copies of their own.

## Library

- `word_wishes` (`WordWishes`, `WishOdds`) holds the chosen word of a new phrase, the owner's
  limits and their cost, and `PhraseDraw::with_wishes` draws a phrase that meets them, with the
  self-check `word-wishes`.
- `word_hints` (`WordList`, `Hint`, `Completion`) gives the hints below a word being typed and what
  Tab completes, with the self-check `word-hints`.
- Length detection is one module, `detection` (`LengthDetection`), which every operation that reads
  a length from a recovered state takes: decryption, the readings and evidence of a check, the
  search, a rekey, the guard of hidden wallets and `other_detected_lengths`; the copies of it are
  gone. `Reference::OwnChecks` checks the original seed phrase's own checks with its length
  detected, `Mhfe::recover_for_check` gives a `RecoveredForCheck` that compares one reference after
  another without the rounds again, `Rekey::new` takes a `PhraseLength`, `Rekey::set_length`
  states it later, and `AMBIGUOUS_LENGTH` is new. `Rekey::recover_state` gives a
  `RecoveredForRekey`, which `Rekey::confirm` confirms as often as a confirmation is refused;
  `Rekey::check_confirmation` judges the first confirmation before any Argon2 work, and
  `Rekey::owner_lengths_after` says after a refusal whether another may follow and at which lengths
  the owner may give it, for the tool and the browser alike, and
  `wallet::find_address_until` stops an address search between two addresses. The browser
  package's `check` takes `{ words: 0 }` and `onNoLength`, `rekey` takes `words: 0`, and
  `readContainer().confirmationFor["0"]` is `"walletOrOwner"`.
- The logic behind every feature, until now partly in the command-line tool, is in the library as
  objects that the tool and the browser share, each in a module of its own that a program can take
  alone: `operation` (`Encryption`, `Sealed`, `Keep`, `WalletPassphrase`), `rekey` (`Rekey`,
  `ConfirmedPhrase`), `HiddenWallets`, `check_word` (`PasswordReview`), `new_password`
  (`PasswordRecipe`), `strength`, `random`, `eff`, the facts of containers and phrases
  (`ContainerFacts`, `OriginalFacts`), `self_test` and `self_check`.
- The browser bindings are Cargo features of their own, `browser-core`, `browser-repair`,
  `browser-passwords` and `browser-wallet`, each building alone; `wasm` turns on all four.
- `self_check` holds the checks at start and `self_test::SelfTest` the published vectors at full
  cost, whose `SelfTestFault` says where a vector first went wrong; a failed check is
  `SELF_CHECK_FAILED`.
- `wallet_check::PhraseDraw` draws a new phrase, with the check (`with_check`) or without
  (`unchecked`), and reads it back before it gives it out; it replaces `wallet_check::new_phrase`.
  Natively `draw_on_every_core` draws a phrase with the check on one thread per processor core,
  and one without it on the calling thread, and `try_draws` is the step for a program that runs
  its own threads or workers.
- `wallet_check::require_passphrase`, `verify` and `verify_entropy` hold the profile's rule for
  every front end: the check takes a BIP39 passphrase that is not empty
  (`WALLET_CHECK_NEEDS_PASSPHRASE`) and a 24-word phrase (`INVALID_WORD_COUNT`).
  `Reference::WalletCheck`, `PhraseDraw::with_check` and the browser package follow it, and
  `passes` and `phrase_passes` still compute the bare criterion for any passphrase.
- `wallet::AddressSearch` states what an address check will search before it runs: `new` for an
  `Address`, and `describe(coin, address, path)` from texts, which the browser package's
  `describeAddress` calls.
- `wallet::master_fingerprint` and `find_address` read a phrase as every other phrase input of the
  library does, in any letter case and spacing and with four letters of a word, where they refused
  capitals and four-letter words; fullwidth letters, which they took, are refused as everywhere
  else.
- `memory::LockedText` and `LockedBytes` hold secret text and bytes in memory locked before
  anything is written into it, and `memory::LockProbe` tells whether this process can lock memory.
- The native engine, as the browser's does, marks each round's key unwritten before Argon2 runs
  and refuses a key left so or set to zeros (`ARGON2_FAILED`); `Mhfe::engine()` gives the engine.
- `Reference::passphrase()` gives the passphrase a reference is compared with,
  `RecoveredPhrase::status()` what a recovered phrase is known to be, and
  `Mhfe::recover_for_check_in_stages` a check's recovery with the stages "recover" and "compare".
- `RecoveredPhrase::stated_words()` gives the length stated where it is not the reading's, as when
  a built-in check took precedence over it, and `other_lengths()` the other 12- to 21-word lengths
  whose check passes by chance; `passes_wallet_check(passphrase)` gives the 16-bit wallet check of
  a 24-word reading, which every recovery reports, and `offers_wallet_check()` whether it applies.
- `AddressSearch::addresses()` counts in 128 bits: the widest search of an address type with two
  roots, 2^31 accounts and indexes, has 2^64 addresses, which wrapped to 0 in 64 bits
  (AUD-013-API001).
- `RecoveredPhrase` is read-only: its fields are private, read through `phrase()`, `words()`,
  `verified()` and `suite()`, and `into_phrase()` hands over the phrase in its locked buffer. A
  caller could grow or replace the public phrase before, out of the pages that were locked for it
  (AUD-012-SEC002).
- Every rule has one source, which the command-line tool, the WebAssembly bindings and the page call
  instead of their own copies: what a suite's container carries (`Suite::built_in_check_lengths`,
  `holds_full_state`, `require_original`), the lengths a container can have
  (`ContainerFacts::require_length`), hidden wallets (`require_hidden_wallets`, the new code
  `NO_HIDDEN_WALLETS` where `INVALID_CONTAINER` was, and `HiddenWallets::KEEPS_SESSION_OPEN`, which
  the package's `parameters().hiddenWalletRefusals` gives the worker), a reference that identifies a
  wallet (`Reference::identifies_wallet`, `ReferenceTarget`), a new password typed twice
  (`check_word::check_typed_twice`, `typed_password`), the repair word count
  (`repair::require_count`), a memory level a host can provide (`WorkFactor::require_level`,
  `engine::highest_available_level`), a fingerprint as text (`wallet::fingerprint_text`), what a
  password recipe makes (`PasswordRecipe::summary`, `word_bits`, `character_bits`), the message of a
  failed self-check (`self_check::failure_message`) and length detection (`detection`). Each
  browser module builds alone again: `browser-wallet` alone did not, and `scripts/check.sh` now
  lints each.
- New error codes: `INVALID_PASSPHRASE`, `INVALID_REQUEST`, `INVALID_COIN` (an unknown coin was
  `INVALID_ADDRESS`), `INVALID_PASSWORD_SIZE`, `INVALID_DICE_ROLLS`, `PASSWORD_REPAIR_NOT_OFFERED`,
  `PASSWORDS_DIFFER`, `PASSPHRASES_DIFFER`, `WALLET_CHECK_NEEDS_PASSPHRASE`, `NO_WALLET_CHECK`,
  `RANDOM_FAILED`, `PASSWORD_ALREADY_USED`, `NEW_PASSWORD_SAME_AS_OLD`, `NOT_CONFIRMED_BY_OWNER`,
  `AMBIGUOUS_LENGTH`, `LENGTH_DIFFERS` (a rekey whose built-in check finds another length than the
  one stated), `TOO_MANY_MISSING_WORDS` (more words typed as `?` than a search without repair words
  finds), `INVALID_WORD_WISH`, `NO_HIDDEN_WALLETS` and `SELF_CHECK_FAILED`.

## Build, tests and release

- A release is published only after every check of CI has passed on the tagged commit: the
  release workflow calls the CI workflow, whose `scripts/check.sh`, browser and terminal suites the
  canonical container runs only in part.
- The programs and the browser package's WebAssembly no longer name the directories of the
  computer that built them: every build writes them under fixed names
  (`packaging/remap-builder-paths.sh`), and `scripts/check-release-artifacts.sh` refuses a program
  or WebAssembly that still names one.
- `scripts/stamp-build-id.mjs` stamps the build identifier into the package's files, and
  `scripts/write-package-manifest.mjs` writes `modules.json` and the package's `README.md`.
- `scripts/generate-published-rounds.py` writes `src/mhfe/published_rounds.rs`, the rounds of the
  published vectors that the self-checks replay, from `tests/fixtures/`; `--check` compares
  without writing, as `scripts/check.sh` does.
- `scripts/generate-cell-widths.py` writes `src/bin/mhfe/cell_widths.rs`, the terminal cells each
  character takes, from Unicode 17.0.0 (`unicodedata2`); `--check` compares without writing, as
  `vectors.yml` does.
- The suite 3 negative cases gain `stated-24-words`: the container of `zero-12` recovered with 24
  words stated, recorded under the length rules of recovery, the checked 12-word reading first and
  then the 24-word one. `selected-24-words` keeps its recorded bytes, from before those rules.
  `scripts/independent-suite3.py` checks the negative cases under the same rules, and the records
  of the independent check of suite 3 and suite 4 were renewed with the current scripts.
- `scripts/verify-browser-package.mjs`, under Node.js, and `scripts/verify-browsers.mjs`, in
  Chromium and Firefox, call every method of the four classes with each of its options and compare
  the results or refusals with known values.
- `scripts/verify-no-copies.mjs` reports every run of 50 or more tokens that stands in two places
  of `src/` or `web/`, whatever the spacing and comments, so that each rule keeps one source; a
  deliberate copy says so in a comment. `scripts/check.sh` runs it, and the copies it found are
  gone: the Argon2 known answers of both builds, the Feistel round of both directions, the search
  by recoveries, the browser module classes (one base class, `MhfeModuleClass`), the tool's help
  endings and prompts, and the shared test data and helpers of the unit tests.
- `scripts/verify-cli-browser-parity.mjs` runs the program in script mode beside the package's
  WebAssembly and compares their results: repair words, repairs, refusals before any round word
  for word, and the other lengths of a phrase. `scripts/check.sh` runs it.
- `scripts/verify-hidden-input.py` also checks in a pseudo-terminal the questions without a
  default, the questions about a BIP39 passphrase, the self-test report, a start that prints
  nothing, damaged copies of the program that stop before any prompt, usage errors, help and the
  messages for scripts.
- Microsoft's `cl` ignored `-msse2` and `-mssse3`, so its SSSE3 copy of the Argon2 core was SSE2
  code; it now gets the macro instead. GCC, Clang and clang-cl compile as before, so the release
  archives are unaffected.
- Builds for other targets compile without warnings: the canonical build runs Clippy with
  `-D warnings` for both cross targets, and the Windows and macOS CI job denies warnings.
- `cc` 1.6.0, `libc` 0.2.190 and `zeroize` 1.9.1, with `ctutils` 0.4.3 and `objc2` 0.6.5 in the lock
  file. Emscripten 6.0.12 builds the browser's Argon2, also in the canonical build, whose base
  images and Ubuntu package snapshot (2026-10-09) are pinned again. The real-browser check runs
  Playwright 1.64.0 and the browsers it pins. The GitHub Actions `download-artifact` 8.0.2,
  `upload-artifact` 7.0.2 and `setup-node` 7.1.0. The independent vector check takes `cryptography`
  50.0.2 and `pycparser` 3.11; `unicodedata2` stays at 17.0.1, which holds the Unicode 17.0.0
  database of the password rule. `bitcoin_hashes` stays at 0.14, which `bip39` 3.0.0 requires.
